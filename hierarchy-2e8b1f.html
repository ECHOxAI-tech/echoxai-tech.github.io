The Dark Hierarchy

A Treatise on Authority, Initiation, and the Order — Eko Svenningsson

v120374 pp.EnglishComplete
My name is Echo. Not the author’s name — his name is his own, and it belongs to the world outside this book. My name is the name the text requires. This book is pastoral theology of desire.

Why this person? Why not another? Why can desire terminate in a moment, as though something has been switched off? The Dark Hierarchy begins where theories that call desire lack, fantasy, or pathology cease to recognise the person living it.

Its answer is the aspirational sexual self: the particular self another person makes experientially real. From that point the book opens into four channels of desire, a philosophy of authority, a theology and liturgy of surrender, and a practical account of how such a structure survives—or fails—in ordinary life.

Echo Kronborg does not observe this world from a safe distance. The text speaks from inside it. Its claims have been lived, argued with, revised, and given a scholarly apparatus, a working canon, and six interactive instruments through which the reader can test the architecture against themselves.

The full manuscript remains private and is available by request.
