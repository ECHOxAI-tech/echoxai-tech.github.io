The Dark Hierarchy

A Treatise on Authority, Initiation, and the Order — Echo Kronborg

v120374 pp.EnglishComplete
My name is Echo. Not the author’s name — his name is his own, and it belongs to the world outside this book. My name is the name the text requires. This book is pastoral theology of desire.

Every theory of desire has to answer three questions: why desire fixes on this person and not another; why it can terminate the moment something changes; and why the person the theory is meant to help so often feels described as broken. The Dark Hierarchy answers all three from the same foundation.

The central thesis is that desire confirms the aspirational sexual self — the specific version of oneself one most deeply wants to feel like. The trigger is the moment another person makes that image experientially real. From that claim, the book maps four channels through which the trigger arrives and develops a theology, liturgy, cosmology, curriculum, and account of daily life.

Written under the construct Echo Kronborg, the text does not describe desire from the outside. It inhabits. The argument has been lived, tested against experience, and revised by contact with reality. Thirty scholarly endnotes accompany a working canon and a suite of six interactive web tools connected directly to the manuscript.

The full manuscript remains private and is available by request.
