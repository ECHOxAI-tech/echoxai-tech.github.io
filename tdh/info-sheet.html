Six readerships, one book

  • Leather, kink and BDSM communities, and households practising hierarchy: the book is written from inside this world.
  • Continental philosophy and critical theory readers, the Semiotext(e) and Repeater constituency: Lacan, Bataille, Bersani, Han, Weil, Foucault.
  • Queer and gender-studies scholars and students: a full theoretical apparatus and glossary, a scholarly engagement with more than 25 named thinkers and traditions, and a 15-week seminar syllabus.
  • Spiritual and pastoral readers: liturgy, canticles, and dark-night material engage Weil, Otto, Turner, Nouwen, and Loder.
  • Cultural programming audiences in Berlin and Northern Europe: festivals, galleries, reading series, and philosophy nights.
  • BDSM-ethnography readers: the apparatus engages Margot Weiss, Gayle Rubin, and Staci Newmahr directly.

Six story angles for press

  • “Against Lacan, from inside the dungeon.” A theory book that tests its argument against lived practice rather than the clinic.
  • A book with a private companion. Seven instruments, local-first storage, no analytics, and an 18+ gate: the opposite of a data-harvesting app.
  • The author audits himself. The book is “not a confession; it is version control,” and its twentieth movement analyses the author’s own relationship record.
  • “God has two neighbors: Order and Humor.” Bergman’s dictum turned into a household philosophy: quotable, funny, serious.
  • Berlin as a leather capital. A natural partner for Folsom Europe week and the city’s fetish and queer cultural calendar.
  • Consent as theology. Revocability and autonomy are stated as preconditions on page one, answering the “is this safe?” question before it is asked.

  • Reading series and philosophy nights built around single movements: the 21-movement structure is modular.
