About

ECHOx

The working name of a Swedish artist based in Berlin, working across fiction, poetry, theater, photography, music, and development.

01 — Biography

Sweden, three disciplines, Berlin

Born in Sweden, with academic formation across mathematics and computer science, religious science, and theater production. The disciplines do not obviously belong together, but each left a working principle: precision must be structural; systems of meaning deserve to be taken seriously; and a work is not complete until it has met its audience.

The move to Berlin was not planned as a creative relocation. It became one. The city has a particular quality of surface — its scars are carried forward rather than concealed, its contradictions unresolved rather than managed. Living in it off and on across several years, then permanently, produced a shift in what felt worth making: work that refused the smoothed-over version, that insisted on the mark of the process, that treated irreversibility not as a limitation but as the condition of honesty.

The practice expanded from fiction into poetry, instant photography, theater, music, and interactive development. ECHOx is the working name across the practice. Eko Svenningsson is the name on the books.

02 — The practice

What survives disappearance

The question that runs through all of the work is this: what survives disappearance? It is not a rhetorical question. It has different answers in different media, and the differences matter.

In writing, the answer is a name, precisely placed, in language that does not decay. The Book of Names is built on that premise: each poem becomes an act of reinstatement, each named subject pulled back from the edge of being forgotten.

In photography, the answer is the irreversible frame. An instant photograph cannot be altered after exposure. What the camera recorded is what existed at that moment—not what the photographer later wished had happened. The constraint is an ethical position, not a technical limitation.

In theater, the answer is more uncomfortable: nothing survives entirely. What remains is what the audience carries out of the room. A dramatic work currently under award consideration remains present here without being disclosed while the decision is pending.

Analog processes, irreversible techniques. Berlin as both ground and metaphysical space. Language as architecture — syntax and rhythm as structural elements, not ornament.
03 — Position

Several forms, one pressure

The mathematical background produces a stubbornness about precision. Psychological states in the fiction become structural problems; surfaces and faces in the photography become geometry; language in the music is treated as architecture rather than decoration. The audience is allowed to arrive at the emotional truth instead of being instructed how to feel.

The disciplines are maintained in parallel and at full commitment. They do not illustrate one another. The fiction and poetry share source material but not form. Photography and theater share irreversibility but not medium. Music and writing share compression but not language. Interactive development takes a final step: an argument becomes a system another person can enter and test.

04 — Current work

Available to read, exhibit, hear, and enter

The manuscript catalogue presents completed and developing books with professional samples and rights information where submission status allows. Faces & Facades and Kiezköter are prepared as venue-ready exhibitions, each supported by a curatorial dossier. Released music, independent productions, and private submission projects are separated clearly. The Dark Hierarchy interactive system demonstrates how the written work can extend into a functioning digital experience.

The work is open to serious conversations about publishing, exhibition, programming, licensing, production, and development collaboration. Each area of the site leads to the relevant material without requiring the visitor to understand the whole practice first.
