The Ones Who Left

A novel in progress — Eko Svenningsson

Planned 95,000–105,000 wordsEnglishSweden / South AfricaIn progress
The first thing Elias Holm thought after his mother died was absurd: she would have hated the lighting. Not death. Not heaven. Not the soul leaving the body. Lighting.

Long before Homo sapiens built cities, another human branch carried its dead into the dark. Homo naledi had a brain that should have made complex ritual easy to dismiss. Yet bodies were placed deep inside a South African cave system, and marks remain on the walls where no daylight reaches. The scientific argument is real. This novel begins where certainty ends.

After his mother’s death, Swedish novelist Elias Holm follows the burial mystery to South Africa and meets Dr. Nandi Maseko, a paleoanthropologist unwilling to let grief pass as evidence. Their disagreement should end there. Instead, something begins answering questions neither of them has asked aloud.

The novel approaches loneliness as both wound and boundary. It asks how much distance a human being can surrender before intimacy becomes something else—and whether the oldest form of recognition may also be the most dangerous.
